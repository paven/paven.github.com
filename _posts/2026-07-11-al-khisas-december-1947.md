**Al-Khisas, Hula Valley, eastern Galilee · night of 18-19 December 1947 · 10-15 killed, among them 5 children · ~470 driven from their homes (most after the raid; the last 50-55 expelled in June 1949) · Palmach night raid that blew up houses over their sleeping residents - months before the State of Israel existed and before any Arab army entered Palestine.**

This is the second post in a series going through, event by event, how Palestinians have lost their lives or homes since 1947. The rules from [the first post about Lifta](/blog/2026/07/11/lifta-december-1947/) still apply: every event carries at least one source a pro-Israel skeptic would accept, and where sources contradict each other I report the contradiction instead of picking the convenient number.

## The village

Al-Khisas lay in the Hula Valley in the far northeast of Galilee, near the Syrian and Lebanese borders. The 1945 British census counted about 530 inhabitants, some 470 of them Arabs, living from farming and fishing on land largely owned by the Emir Faour family. It was a small place with no strategic weight of its own.

## What happened

On the night of 18-19 December 1947, troops of the Palmach's 3rd Battalion - the elite strike force of the Haganah - raided al-Khisas in retaliation for the killing of a Jewish man near a neighboring kibbutz days earlier. The raiders blew up houses in the dark with their residents inside.

The two main historians differ on the toll, and honesty requires showing it:

- Benny Morris (*The Birth of the Palestinian Refugee Problem Revisited*): **10 Arab civilians killed, including 5 children**.
- Ilan Pappe (*The Ethnic Cleansing of Palestine*): **15 villagers killed, including 5 children**.

Both agree on the five children. Both agree the victims were civilians. And this time the perpetrator is not in dispute: it was the Palmach, and the raid is documented in the Haganah's own records as used by Morris.

What makes al-Khisas unusual is the paper trail at the top - though here too the sourcing must be stated precisely. Morris documents that the raid was severely criticized within the Jewish leadership: the Arab Division of the Jewish Agency's Political Department condemned it for having "unnecessarily spread the fighting to a hitherto quiet area." The stronger claim - that David Ben-Gurion denied the raid had been authorized, publicly apologized, and then a few months later included al-Khisas in a list of successful operations - comes from Ilan Pappe (The Ethnic Cleansing of Palestine, p. 57), and I have not found it independently corroborated by Morris. Pappe's handling of sources has been sharply criticized, by Morris among others, so treat the apology-turned-success-story as Pappe's reading until I can verify it against the primary record. The criticism of the raid inside the Yishuv leadership, per Morris, stands on its own.

Note the date. The raid came three weeks after the UN partition vote, five months before Israel's declaration and the entry of any Arab army. Whatever set al-Khisas's villagers in motion, it was not an Arab invasion.

## What happened afterwards

Much of the population fled after the raid. Morris dates the main depopulation to 25 May 1948. But al-Khisas was not finally emptied even then: some 50-55 villagers stayed, or came back, and lived in the village for over a year under the new state - until the night of 5-6 June 1949, when the Israeli army expelled them and trucked them to 'Akbara. That is a year after the war in the north had ended, and it is worth stating plainly: the last people of al-Khisas did not flee a war, they were removed by a state at peace.

Kibbutz HaGoshrim was established on the village lands in September 1948. The former manor house of Emir Faour operates as part of a hotel today.
